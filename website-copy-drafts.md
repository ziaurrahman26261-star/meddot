### 5.3 Why mental health billing is different (3–4 paragraphs, real depth)
```
Draft (write fresh, verify current rules before publish):

Mental health billing fails in predictable places — and all of them are
fixable. Psychotherapy codes (90834, 90837) come with frequency and medical
necessity rules payers enforce aggressively. Add-on codes (90833, 90836,
90838) get rejected when the E/M service isn't separately documented.
Telehealth billing depends on getting the modifiers (95, GT) and place-of-
service right — one wrong field and the claim denies.

Then there are the provider-specific rules: LCSWs, LMFTs, and PsyDs all have
different Medicare billing privileges, and group vs. individual practice
arrangements change what you can bill and how. Commercial plans add their
own medical-necessity and out-of-network policies.

This is our daily work. MEDDOT SOLUTIONS's mental health team tracks these rules
per payer, audits your claims before submission, and fixes denials at the
root — so the same claim never denies twice.
```

### 5.4 Top 5 mental health denials (table) + how we fix them
```
| Denial reason | Why it happens | How we prevent it |
|---|---|---|
| "Diagnosis not covered" | F-codes vs. payer's medical necessity policy | Pre-submission policy check per payer |
| "Missing/invalid modifier" | Telehealth claims without 95/GT or wrong POS | Automated modifier validation |
| "Frequency exceeded" | 90837 billed too often without justification | Visit-log tracking + prior-auth alerts |
| "Not medically necessary" | Progress notes don't support the code | Charting checklists for your notes |
| "Service not covered by plan" | Out-of-network or plan exclusions | Eligibility verified before the visit |
```
